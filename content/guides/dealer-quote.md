## Ask for the full on-road breakup

An ex-showroom figure is not the amount you pay to put a new car on the road. Registration, road tax, insurance and optional accessories can change the total, and the applicable charges vary by state and city.

Ask the dealer to separate the vehicle price, registration and tax, insurance, accessories, discounts and any handling or logistics line. Request the same variant, fuel and transmission in every quote, and ask which items are optional.

Manufacturer price pages also warn that prices can vary by location and change over time. For example, [Hyundai's CRETA price page](https://www.hyundai.com/in/en/find-a-car/creta/price) lets buyers select a model, state and city and advises confirming current prices with a dealer.

## Compare what is actually included

1. Match the exact variant and model year.
2. Compare the final payable total, not just the starting price.
3. Separate one-time purchase costs from recurring insurance and maintenance.
4. Get any promised discount or accessory in writing.
5. Keep your own quote; an estimate is not a booking or a guarantee of final charges.

If two offers still differ, ask each dealer to explain the line items before paying a booking amount.
